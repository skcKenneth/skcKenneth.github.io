TEACHER REFERENCE · PUBLIC PAGE
Sampling: does more mean
better? — Teacher reference
Prepare the lesson, inspect the reasoning and keep the evidence limits visible.
45 minutes · Junior secondary · pairs or groups of 2‒4
Before you begin
Frequency, percentages and reading a bar chart. No programming required.
An authored 45-minute sequence using an existing synthetic inquiry. The 200-person school
and its activity group are invented, not a real school survey.
Synthetic starting counts: the whole school has 200 people, with 100 choosing A; the activity group
has 40 people, with 36 choosing A. These fixed counts support a paper comparison and are not a
newly drawn sample.
Check that the sampling activity opens. Arrange an operator, recorder and questioner; every
learner writes an individual prediction first.
Keep the chart axis at 0% for the main comparison. The synthetic school has 100 A responses
out of 200; the group has 36 out of 40.
One-class sequence
5 min 01. Predict before asking
Will asking all 40 group members be closer to the school’s 50% than asking 20 randomly
chosen pupils? Explain before running.
Teacher reasoning / look for
Collect reasons before displaying results. Distinguish “more people” from “different
people have a chance to be selected”.

--- Page 2 ---
12 min 02. Keep size, change who is asked
Use the selected seed (default 2026) to compare school-20 and club-20. Record the A count,
sample size and percentage. Save both pieces of evidence in the original tool.
Teacher reasoning / look for
Only the sampling frame changes. A sample’s percentage uses its own sample size as
the denominator, not 200.
10 min 03. Increase size within each frame
Compare club-40 and school-40 with the earlier cases. Which pupils are still excluded by
asking only the group?
Teacher reasoning / look for
The full group gives 36/40 = 90%, while the full school is 50%. Surveying that entire
group removes within-group sampling variability but does not fix its mismatch with the
school.
10 min 04. Draw again and revise
Use the next-seed control and inspect the listed 30 repeats. Revise “more people always
means more accurate”. Do not pick only the nicest result.
Teacher reasoning / look for
The 30 repeats begin at the displayed seed. They describe these deterministic draws, not
all possible surveys. Larger random samples generally reduce variability; a single draw
need not improve.

--- Page 3 ---
8 min 05. Plan a lunch survey
A pupil asks only the basketball team about favourite lunches. Propose a better sampling
frame, note possible non-response, then answer the exit question.
Teacher reasoning / look for
Look for a whole-grade sampling frame and opportunities across classes/interests. An
improved plan still cannot promise an exact population percentage.
Keep this evidence
Two saved comparisons with method, size, seed and A percentage.
A revised claim and a lunch-survey plan with one limitation.
Use Save evidence, Download record and Print in the original inquiry. Its local record stays in
this browser; this classroom page does not read, save or import it. Paper responses can be
kept separately.
Exit question
If the complete activity group gives 90%, can we claim the whole school is 90%? Why?
Reference reasoning
No. The observed group is not the whole school. Here the invented school proportion is
50%; increasing the group sample does not include the other pupils.
Misconceptions to discuss
A bigger sample automatically fixes selection bias.
A lucky random draw proves the sampling method is always better.
Changing the chart axis changes the observations.
These statements are misconceptions, not conclusions. Ask for the missing condition or evidence.
